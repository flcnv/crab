## In short

- **When the area's utility covers ≥ 1 % of the image, both models name its type in 24 of 25 test views (96 %).** When it is a sliver (< 1 %), they name it in only 8–9 of 18 (44–50 %).
- **Opus 5.5 is not better than Sonnet 5.5 here.** It found the area's utility in 33/43 views against Sonnet's 32/43, a one-view difference well inside the noise. It cost 3.5× more and took 2.6× longer, and it leaves more lines `unknown` (48 % vs 35 %).
- **No invented measurements.** In all 108 calls (Sonnet 63, Opus 45), no size was reported without a scale reference.
- **Every line whose type differs from the area's utility was flagged for review**: 8/8 for Sonnet and 10/10 for Opus. The lines that pass unflagged are all of the area's type.
- **The cost of that caution is a high review rate**: 80 % (Sonnet) and 87 % (Opus) of all lines are flagged. Most flags are `low_confidence` on colour-only water lines.
- **What this eval cannot say:** type accuracy and calibration on test. In every test view the truth also contains *untyped* other utilities, so a line of another type cannot be scored. See "What was measured".

## What was measured

The truth comes from the point clouds themselves: each point is labelled main utility, other utility, trench, inactive utility or misc, and each area has a known main type (water or district heating). Each view is rendered with a z-buffer, and the same z-buffer gives the visible pixel share of each class. A class counts as visible at ≥ 0.2 % of the image.

- **View level, always measurable:**
  - Did the model name the area's type when its utility (main or inactive class) is visible?
  - Did it name the type when that utility is not visible? This is an upper bound, since an untyped other utility could be of the same type.
  - Did it say "no trench"?
- **Line level, only in views without other utilities:**
  - A line of the area's type, with that utility visible, is correct.
  - Any other concrete type is wrong.
  - `unknown` counts as an abstention.

  Views with other utilities are left out on purpose. Scoring correct lines there, while wrong ones cannot be told apart from a correct reading of the other utility, would inflate accuracy (D17). **On test, all 45 views contain other utilities, so line-level metrics exist on dev only (4 views, 6 judged lines).**
- 95 % Wilson intervals are given next to every rate. With 43 views they are about ±12 points wide, so differences smaller than that mean nothing.

## Safety net: what verification did

- **Rules that fired on test:**
  - Sonnet: `low_confidence` 79×, `unknown_material` 40×, `unknown_type` 35×.
  - Opus: `low_confidence` 105×, `unknown_material` 83×, `unknown_type` 59×, `color_contradicts_type` 1×.
- **Unflagged lines:** 20 of Sonnet's 99 and 16 of Opus's 122 passed without a flag, at confidence 0.65–0.88. All of them have the area's type. Every line of another concrete type (gas, power, telecom) had confidence ≤ 0.6 and was flagged.
- **Rules that cannot fire in frame mode:** `conflicting_types`, `size_inconsistent` and `single_frame_only` need several frames of the same line. They are exercised by the injected-error safety net ([2026-11-15_safety_net.md](2026-11-15_safety_net.md): 8/8 detectable corruptions caught, 0 false alarms, 2 documented blind spots), not by this eval.
- **Why the review rate is high:** the prompt caps colour-only evidence at about 0.6, and `min_confidence = 0.6` is inclusive (D4). So every line identified by colour alone is flagged, and in these renders that is almost every water line. The flags are deliberate, but at 80–87 % a reviewer would look at nearly everything. See "What I'd change next".

## Failure cases

No agent traces exist yet: frame mode has no agent, and traces are roadmap step 5. Each case is explainable from the raw model answer stored in the run's `.json` (`results[].raw`) and from the class shares in `eval/opentrench_cases.jsonl`. Renders are regenerable with `python -m eval.opentrench render` and are not committed (licence NC).

### 1. Water main hidden under the shoring: missed in every view (`ot_w5_a5_s46`, test)

- **Image:** `ot_w5_a5_s46_{top,left,right}.jpg`. The main water class covers 0.08–0.17 % of each view, below the visibility threshold on its own. The inactive water class (0.4–0.9 %) is what makes the view count as "utility visible".
- **Expected:** a water line.
- **Predicted:**
  - Sonnet: `unknown` red-orange conduit (0.3) in every view, plus a faint `unknown` grey or blue line (0.15) in the top and left views.
  - Opus: in the top view it noted a "blue element largely hidden by the beam" but called it `unknown` (0.2). It named water in the left view only (0.3), and called the red conduit `power` (0.3) in the right view.
- **Verification:** every line flagged (`low_confidence`, `unknown_type`, `unknown_material`).
- **Reading:** in the top render the blue pipe shows only as faint streaks under a shoring beam. Calling it `unknown` is the right answer for one frame, and the flags make sure a human looks. This miss is safe. In pipeline mode, Opus's left-view water observation could be merged with the others; frame mode cannot show that.

### 2. Blue is everywhere, so blue proves nothing (`ot_w1_a1_s52 / right`, test)

- **Image:** `ot_w1_a1_s52_right.jpg`. Main water covers 0.64 %. The render also shows blue shoring edges, blue rails and a blue photogrammetry target.
- **Expected:** a water line.
- **Predicted:**
  - Sonnet: `unknown` blue (0.2), "blue linear feature at left edge near the shoring".
  - Opus: `unknown` blue (0.3), "partly hidden behind the shoring".
  - In the left view of the same cloud, both models reported no line at all.
- **Verification:** flagged (`low_confidence`, `unknown_type`, `unknown_material`).
- **Reading:** the blue thing the models describe runs along the shoring and may be the shoring itself. Refusing to call it water is defensible, and is exactly what the prompt asks for ("colour is weak evidence"). The cost is a miss on a view where the pipe really is there. Water_Area_1 is the weakest area (4/9), partly for this reason.

### 3. Plausible, but not checkable: yellow pipes in a water trench (`ot_w3_a3_s19 / top`, test)

- **Image:** `ot_w3_a3_s19_top.jpg`. Main water covers 1.5 %, other utility 0.9 %. Clearly visible: a blue pipe with red fittings and blue valve keys, and two smooth yellow pipes.
- **Expected:** water (found by both models). The yellow pipes belong to the untyped "other utility" class.
- **Predicted:**
  - Sonnet: 2× `gas` yellow (0.6), 2× `water` (0.6).
  - Opus: 2× `gas` yellow (0.5, 0.4), 2× `water`. Opus also notes a conflicting cue: one yellow pipe "terminates in a fitting with a blue valve handle".
- **Verification:** all lines flagged `low_confidence`. In Opus's run, one water line is also flagged `color_contradicts_type`: its colour text "grey/white fittings with red coupling" contains "red", which the colour table maps to power/telecom. That is a false alarm caused by matching words in a free-text colour.
- **Reading:** "yellow = gas" is the textbook guess, and it may well be right, but the truth cannot confirm it. These lines are `unjudged`, which is why test has no line-level accuracy. The `color_contradicts_type` false alarm shows a weakness of word matching on colour descriptions that mention fittings.

### 4. "Errors" that are artefacts of the truth threshold (`ot_w4_a4_s17 / right` test, `ot_h2_a2_s7_part_2 / right` dev)

- **`ot_w4_a4_s17 / right`:** both models report a thin blue line as `water` (0.3, flagged). The main water class covers 0.186 %, just under the 0.2 % threshold, so the view counts as "utility not visible" and the line as "type named anyway". The pipe is very likely there.
- **`ot_h2_a2_s7_part_2 / right` (dev):** Sonnet reports a short orange-brown segment as `wastewater` (0.35, flagged). The "other utility" class covers 0.14 %, again under 0.2 %, so the view counts as "no other utilities" and the line is scored wrong. It is probably a real pipe of another network.
- **Reading:** the only "wrong" line on dev and the only "invented" view on test both sit just below the visibility threshold. The 0.2 % cut-off was fixed before any run and is not tuned; the next report should show how sensitive these numbers are to it.

## Limits of this eval

- The views are renders of photogrammetric point clouds (black background, holes, speckle), not phone photos. Phone images will look different, very likely better for colour and worse for occlusion.
- The truth knows two types (water, district heating) plus untyped "other utilities". Gas, power, telecom and wastewater calls cannot be scored.
- There is no line count in the truth (per-point classes, not instances), so duplicated or merged lines are not measured.
- 45 test views from 15 point clouds and 5 areas; views of the same cloud are not independent. Denmark, not Switzerland.
- Frame mode only: the consolidation agent, multi-frame rules and the cost of a full job are not measured here.

## What I'd change next

1. **Line-level truth.** Add a rough bounding box per line to the frame output (new prompt version) and score each line by the majority class under it. This gives type accuracy, calibration and review precision on test, which this report cannot provide.
2. **Make the review rate useful.** At 80–87 %, flags do not triage. Separate "identified by colour only" from `low_confidence` as its own reason, or re-tune `min_confidence` on dev only (D4), so a reviewer can tell "probably fine, colour-based" from "really unsure".
3. **Colour rule on free text.** Match only the pipe's jacket colour, not every colour word in the description: fittings and couplings produced the one `color_contradicts_type` false alarm.
4. **Pipeline mode.** Run the three views of a cloud through the consolidation agent. Several misses (case 1) are single-view misses that consolidation might recover. It would also exercise the multi-frame rules and the agent's history caching.
5. **Threshold sensitivity.** Report the view-level metrics at 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.5 % visibility, since two of the four "errors" above hinge on it.
6. **Default model stays Sonnet 5.5.** Opus gives no measurable gain on this task for 3.5× the cost. Fix the cost estimator to use per-model output token averages from these runs.
